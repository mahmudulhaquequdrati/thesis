# Limitations

The absolute numbers in this thesis are contaminated, censored and under-powered. The comparisons are not, because every comparison holds the problem set, the grading and the pricing constant, and the biases that remain act against the direction claimed rather than for it. Each limitation below is stated as the fact, what it damages, and what survives. None appears here for the first time; this chapter collects them.

**1. Not a new routing algorithm.** Route-To-Reason, DART and CodeRouterBench occupy that space. *Damages:* any claim to a novel router, and the proposal's title. *Survives:* the measurement of headroom (13.8 points) and the decomposition of why a modal-label router captures none of it, neither of which is in the prior work.

**2. Not novel on overthinking.** ThoughtTerminator, SelfBudgeter and RecurGuard already study non-termination. *Damages:* any claim to discovering it. *Survives:* the economic framing, dollars per correct answer across a roster spanning two orders of magnitude, and the per-model attribution of the waste.

**3. Not contamination-controlled.** LiveCodeBench's problems date from 2024-09-22 to 2025-04-06; the dataset stopped updating on 2025-06-05; every roster model is a 2026 release. There is no post-cutoff window. *Damages:* absolute pass rates on LiveCodeBench; a high score on a hard problem may be memorisation. *Survives:* every comparison is between configurations on the same problems, and contamination inflates all configurations on a given problem roughly equally, so it cannot generate the 25.7-point within-style gap between effort arms. Each problem's contest date is stored so exposure can be quantified. No public code benchmark ships 2026 problems with tests; building one would take months.

**4. Not fully powered.** Comparable statistics rest on 60–107 problems; per-model thinking arms are 18–36 calls. Five adjacent CPC pairs and the `flash|high` / `pro|high` accuracy pair have overlapping intervals. *Damages:* individual orderings between adjacent configurations. *Survives:* the large effects: a 25.7-point style-matched gap on hard problems, a 65× CPC spread on an identical exam, an exact 64/68 tie between the cheap and the frontier model. Directions are clear; fine orderings are marked as not established.

**5. Not a tier-level story.** The per-tier aggregate averages models whose responses to reasoning have opposite signs. *Damages:* every aggregate off-versus-on row, including the "+29.3 on hard" headline. *Survives, and is stronger:* the value of reasoning is a property of the (model, difficulty) pair, and the two failure modes behind a negative delta are separated by the censoring column.

**6. Not style-balanced.** The run order's string tie-break made the thinking arm almost entirely function-style on LiveCodeBench (hard: 8 stdin, 110 functional). *Damages:* the raw tier-level comparison and the representativeness of the 60-problem frontier set (51 functional, 2 stdin). *Survives:* the within-style effect on both tiers (+25.7 hard, +28.9 medium), and the frontier, oracle, headroom and router results as statements about function-style problems. *Cannot be claimed:* anything about stdin-style problems; that arm is n=8.

**7. Not free of censoring.** 26.3% of hard thinking calls truncate at 48,000 tokens. *Damages:* the long-reasoning tail; the 54.2% hard-tier pass rate and the 39% band are floors. *Survives:* the direction, because censoring biases the thinking arm downward, so the central finding is understated rather than overstated. A bias that works against one's own claim is the safest kind to have.

**8. The grid is unbalanced, and not at random.** The `off` arm is complete for three models; the `high` arm covers 73–104 problems; the held-out model 16–23. The missing cells are the *later* problems in the run order. *Damages:* any per-configuration statistic over that configuration's own problems, and the "solved by nothing" bucket of the problem-level split. *Survives:* every reported comparison uses a shared set and prints its denominator. Balancing the thinking arm would have cost about $1.50 and required raising the $6.00 cap; it was recorded as a limitation instead, deliberately.

**9. Costs are computed, not billed, for the grid.** Only the 139 pilot rows carry the aggregator's billed figure; the 1,224 grid rows are token counts × the pinned contracted price. *Damages:* the claim "every call priced from the actual bill", which is withdrawn. *Survives:* with fallbacks disabled the pinned price is contractual, and the pilot's 1.354× gap is best read as evidence for the pinning finding. The reconciliation is free and is the first action owed.

**10. The reasoning traces were never stored.** The harness stores the visible answer; the reasoning text the aggregator returns was never read, and no column holds it. *Damages:* `reasoning_tokens` is a vendor annotation that nothing in the pipeline can check, and the overthinking finding can be counted but not inspected: nobody can say what the 49 wasted calls were doing for 29,584 tokens. Unlike an extraction bug, this is not recoverable offline. *Survives:* every count and cost rests on the same vendor field that the bill rests on, so the economics are internally consistent even if the mechanism is opaque. This is the first thing to change in a re-run, and it would cost nothing extra.

**11. Determinism is assumed, not verified.** One sample per cell at temperature 0; `temperature_sent` records what was sent, not what the endpoint applied; and with one sample nondeterminism cannot be detected. Given that two other parameters were silently ignored (Chapter 4), this assumption is not a safe one. *Damages:* any claim that a pass is reliable rather than lucky. *Survives:* the design choice bought half the cost and a deterministic routing label, and every configuration was treated identically.

**12. Two of the router's three features are benchmark metadata.** Difficulty is the benchmark's label; test count is the hidden suite's size. *Damages:* the proposal's "features of the incoming prompt" positioning, independently of the router's result, and the strength of "feature insufficiency 0.0". *Survives:* the oracle, hull and headroom, which use no features.

**13. RQ5 is under-powered.** The held-out model ran on 16–23 problems. *Damages:* any transfer claim. *Survives:* honestly, little; it is reported as under-powered rather than as a number.

**14. Three families, five models, single-file Python.** Two DeepSeek, two Qwen, one Moonshot; no closed-weight reference; no multi-language, repository-scale or agentic tasks. *Damages:* generality. *Survives:* everything claimed is scoped to open-weight code generation on these benchmarks.

**15. The literature assessment is not from full reads.** Claims about Route-To-Reason, HRBench and Agent-as-a-Router rest on abstracts, summaries and PDF extraction (Appendix I). *Damages:* the precision of the "what it does not do" column. *Survives:* the checkable claim about CodeRouterBench's released data, which was verified against the dataset's columns.
